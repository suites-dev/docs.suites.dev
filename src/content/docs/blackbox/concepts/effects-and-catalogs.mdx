## From Observation to Finding

An **effect** is a normalized fact about supported behavior at a system boundary during one runtime attempt. Depending on the available proof, an effect can show that an operation was observed, succeeded, or committed.

OpenTelemetry provides runtime observations. Blackbox admits only supported, attributable observations and normalizes them into portable effects. A span is evidence input, not automatically an effect.

Runtime records facts. It does not decide whether those facts are acceptable. A person approves effect policy, and the Analyzer evaluates the accepted claim against the evidence.

## Use the Effects Catalog as Shared Vocabulary

An **effects catalog** is a reviewed collection of portable effect contracts. It connects OpenTelemetry observations to stable product intent without tying accepted behavior to raw span IDs or trace structure.

Each catalog entry defines the intent represented by an effect, when an observation qualifies, and which proof strengths it can support. Accepted behavior then references that shared intent.

The public syntax is not frozen. This YAML is an illustrative catalog view, not a supported file format or schema:

```yaml title="Illustrative effects catalog"
effects:
  payment-charge-request:
    intent: Payment service received a charge request
    matches:
      capability: io.http.client
      operation: request
      target: payment-service
      url: https://payment-service/charges
    supports: [observed, succeeded]

  subscription-record-written:
    intent: A subscription record was inserted
    matches:
      capability: datastore.sql.client
      operation: insert
      target: subscriptions
      query: INSERT INTO subscriptions (customer_id, plan_id) VALUES (?, ?)
    supports: [observed, succeeded, committed]
```

The catalog supplies the shared intents. A claim such as “at most one charge per completed order” remains separate, human-approved policy.

| Runtime input                                                     | Catalog intent             | Accepted claim                         |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Two attributable HTTP client observations at the payment boundary | Two charge-request effects | At most one charge per completed order |

The catalog defines the middle column. A person approves the claim on the right. Runtime observations cannot add catalog entries, redefine their intent, or turn themselves into policy.

## Required and Forbidden Effects

- A **required effect** describes behavior that must occur.
- A **forbidden effect** describes behavior that must not occur.
- An unlisted effect remains evidence, but it does not constrain the implementation.

Observed behavior can inform a proposal. It never becomes accepted policy without human approval.

## Positive Facts and Absence Need Different Evidence

| Claim                   | What positive facts can establish   | What needs bounded completeness        |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **At least N**          | Satisfaction after N matching facts | Violation below N                      |
| **At most N**           | Violation after more than N facts   | Satisfaction at or below N             |
| **Exactly N**           | Violation after more than N facts   | Satisfaction at N or violation below N |
| **Forbidden or absent** | Violation after one matching fact   | Satisfaction with zero matches         |

If evidence is incomplete, Blackbox cannot establish a completeness-dependent absence, upper-bound, or exactness result. The finding stays inconclusive instead of turning missing evidence into success.

Managed capture starts fresh for every attempt, retry, and sequence member. Blackbox does not merge their evidence to strengthen a claim.

## What Deterministic Assurance Means

The same accepted claim, declared realization, analysis policy, and sealed evidence produce the same finding. This does not mean the application will behave identically on every run.

Findings remain distinct:

- **Satisfied:** The evidence meets the claim's proof burden.
- **Violated:** The evidence establishes a counterexample or missed obligation.
- **Inconclusive:** The available evidence cannot support the required conclusion.
- **Unsupported:** The declared capability or realization is unavailable.
- **Integrity failure:** Identities or lifecycle evidence conflict with the claim.

## Observable Decision Coverage Stays Separate

**Observable Decision Coverage (ODC)** is an Alpha target that compares declared decision outcomes with projected effects. It cannot alter an ordinary effect finding or strengthen incomplete effect evidence.
